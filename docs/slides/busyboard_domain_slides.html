The Busyboard Domain

Buttons, lamps, and wiring the robot never sees

pybullet_busyboard - branch bridge-learning
Assets: docs/envs/busyboard/

Why another domain?

  • Every other domain in the suite hides numbers: fan wind, boil heat, glue cure time, domino friction. The shape of the hidden process is known and a few knobs are fitted.
  • Busyboard hides structure: which button feeds which lamp. No setting of a continuous parameter vector expresses it.
  • Some lamps need two buttons at once (an interlock). Earlier busyboard benchmarks for robot learning left that out on purpose to keep the board easy to read from random play.
  • Evidence arrives late: a lamp only lights after its buttons have been held for a while, so the button pressed most recently is often not the one that caused the light.
  • Some buttons do nothing, and every goal asks for some lamp to stay dark. "Press everything" is therefore not a strategy.

The world

  • A Panda arm faces a children's busy board resting on the table.
  • Buttons (front): toggle switches. Pushed from one side they latch on, from the other off. The pale slider's position is the button's state.
  • Lamps (back): grey blocks that glow in their own colour as they light. Each bulb stands on a base in that colour, which says which lamp it is while it is dark. The robot never touches them.
  • Every button and lamp has a fixed colour, carried in the state as a color feature. The red button is the same control on every board, so a rule can be stated as "the yellow lamp needs the green button".
  • Three buttons fit in one row. Every board here has at least four, so two rows.
  • The only things the robot can do: press a button, release a button, or wait.

What the board hides

  • Each lamp has a drive condition: two buttons that must both be on ("green & blue"). One button alone never lights anything.
  • While the condition holds, a hidden charge builds up. When it stops holding, the charge drains, faster than it built.
  • What the camera sees is brightness. It stays at exactly zero for the first ~60% of the charge and only then ramps up. A lamp that is half-way to lighting looks identical to a dead one.
  • Example: the five-button, four-lamp board used in all the videos (seed 0).

  • Undoing is cheap: release a button and its lamp is dark before the next press finishes.
  • Waiting is a real action. A lamp only lights while the board is left alone in a driving configuration.

Goals: light these, keep those dark

  • Every board starts fully off: all buttons released, all lamps dark and uncharged.
  • A goal is a lit/dark assignment over the lamps, e.g. "Use the buttons to leave the cyan lamp (lamp1), the magenta lamp (lamp2) lit and the yellow lamp (lamp0), the white lamp (lamp3) dark."
  • Goals are drawn only from assignments that some button setting exactly realizes, so every task is solvable.
  • With two or more lamps, at least one lamp must stay dark. That off-target is the whole point.
  • Test goals ask for at least two lamps lit (busyboard_min_lit_test = 2): two learned conditions composed, with any button they share held. Training goals may ask for one.

Latch everything and wait. Lights all four lamps. The yellow and white lamps were supposed to stay dark, so the task fails.

State, predicates, skills

Objects and features

  • robot: x, y, z, fingers, roll, tilt, wrist
  • button: x, y, z, rot, color, is_on
  • lamp: x, y, z, rot, color, brightness (+ charge only when fully observable)
  • color is an index into a named palette: buttons red, green, blue, orange, purple, black; lamps yellow, cyan, magenta, white. Fixed per object on every board.

Predicates (all visible to the agent)

  • ButtonOn(b), ButtonOff(b)
  • LampOn(l), LampOff(l): brightness ≥ 0.5
  • HandEmpty(r)

Skills (shared factories, no board-specific motion code)

  • PressButton(r, b): push the slider to the on side
  • ReleaseButton(r, b): push it back
  • Wait(r): hold still

Partially observable run (--partially_observable True)

  • The charge feature is dropped. The agent sees only brightness and has to postulate the accumulator itself.
  • Same shape as boil's hidden heat behind bubbling_level, but here the latent sits behind an unknown wiring.

Train / test split

ButtonsLampsConditions per lampSetting
Train4310busyboard_num_{buttons,lamps}_train
Test5 or 6415 or 21busyboard_num_{buttons,lamps}_test
  • Test boards are bigger than anything seen in training, and they extend the training board: every training lamp keeps exactly its training drive condition, the way glue chemistry or fan thrust is the same at test in the other domains.
  • What a test board adds is either a decoy button or a new lamp whose driver is one of the new buttons. Goals only ever ask for that new lamp to stay dark, so the agent must leave the buttons it knows nothing about alone.
  • One wiring per seed (busyboard_fixed_wiring = True), sampled over the largest board (6 buttons, 4 lamps) under that contract and projected down by keeping the first lamps and folding new-button indices into the buttons a board has.
  • Every lamp is an interlock (busyboard_interlock_prob = 1.0), so every goal needs a combination of buttons. Two lamps are never wired identically, so every lamp can be told apart by some experiment.
  • Four training buttons, not three: three buttons have exactly three pairs, a three-lamp board would use them all, and no button setting could light two lamps without the third. Six pairs leave room for two-lit goals with an off-target.
  • Test goals light at least two lamps (busyboard_min_lit_test = 2); training goals may light one.
  • Board images: init_4buttons_3lamps.png (train size), init_5buttons_4lamps.png, init_6buttons_4lamps.png (test sizes).

Why this split, and why colours

  • The problem with the first draft. Wiring was projected onto smaller boards by reducing button indices modulo the button count, so the training lamps' conditions were different on every test board in 5/5 seeds. A learner that got training exactly right still failed test, and test-time attempts never touch the real board, so nothing could correct it.
  • Two coherent designs. A: the wiring is the hidden physics, fixed for a run; test extends the board (chosen). B: the wiring is a per-episode latent; train varies it too, and the agent synthesizes a closed-loop policy that identifies the board before acting. B is the richer claim and needs a belief simulator that can represent an unknown wiring, which nothing does yet. It stays available as busyboard_fixed_wiring = False.
  • Switch identity is part of the rule. In fan and boil a rule is lifted over features ("the switch on this side drives this fan"). Here nothing about a button says which lamp it feeds, so the rule has to name the button. Giving every button and lamp a fixed color feature makes that rule lifted again: "the yellow lamp needs the green and blue buttons" is stated over features and is true on every board.
  • Colour never leaks the wiring. It is identity only, the same on every board; which colours feed which lamp is still the thing to learn.

How much is there to find out?

  • Per lamp, the candidate conditions are: any single button, or any unordered pair.

  • Small enough to enumerate exactly, so information gain per experiment can be scored in bits against an optimal splitter.
  • A conjunction is symmetric: "b0 and b2" and "b2 and b0" are one hypothesis, not two. With every lamp an interlock the singles drop out: 120 three-lamp wirings on the training board.
  • The useful experiments are the ones that hold a configuration still long enough for the ramp to show. Covering every button once is not enough; the interlock and the delay both confound it.

The oracle: how the demonstrator plans

  • Pressing a button has no fixed effect, so a STRIPS operator cannot describe it. The oracle uses processes instead.
  • Two helper predicates, given to oracle approaches only: SoleDriver(b, l) and JointDrivers(b1, b2, l). They reconstruct the wiring from the board size, so the planner and the env cannot drift apart.
  • Exogenous processes: LightLampSole, LightLampJoint (condition: all drive buttons on, held for the delay), plus the matching darkening processes.
  • Endogenous: PressButton, ReleaseButton, Wait.
  • Result: 10/10 test tasks with oracle_process_planning and --sesame_check_expected_atoms False.

Goal: cyan and magenta lamps lit, yellow and white lamps dark. Plan: press the red, green and blue buttons, then wait.

  • The visibility contract is structural: the base file carries geometry and button mechanics and nothing else. Wiring, charge law, task distribution and goal thresholds live only in the subclass, which the agent never reads.
  • The true wiring is recorded in each task's offline_task_metrics, an experimenter-only channel, so runs can score how much of the wiring an agent actually recovered.

Is it ready to run? What to expect

  • Ready: env tests pass (11/11), oracle solves 10/10 at the 4/3 to 5-6/4 sizes, config wired into envs/all.yaml and exp_busyboard.yaml.
  • Oracle hybrid-sim arm (agent_oracle_hybrid_sim): should solve. Its params carry the max-board wiring and project it onto any board, and it has already captured a Wait-based 5-step plan end to end.
  • Learning arms (sim_predicator, agent_sim_learning): a model that gets the training wiring right is right on every test board. What test adds is more buttons to ignore, a lamp to keep dark, and goals that need two conditions at once. On the earlier 3/2 to 4-5/2-3 split, sim_predicator solved its test task from cycle 1 on (job 21869973).
  • What the agent has to learn is discrete: which coloured buttons feed which lamp, and that a lamp charges only while its whole condition holds. The continuous fitter cannot recover that (the wiring params are a staircase with zero gradient almost everywhere); the agent has to write it into its model itself, from designed experiments.
  • Test-time attempts run against the agent's own simulator, not the real board, so a wrong wiring is never corrected before the one real execution. Getting it right in training is the whole game.

Held in reserve: busyboard_fixed_wiring = False draws a fresh wiring per episode. That is the setting where a synthesized closed-loop policy would identify the board before committing, and it needs the belief simulator to represent an unknown wiring, which nothing does yet.

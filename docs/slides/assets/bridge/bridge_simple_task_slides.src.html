The Bridge Domain

Glue construction with a hidden curing process
(simple variant walkthrough)

pybullet_bridge · envs/pybullet_bridge.py · ground_truth_models/bridge/

Walkthrough of the simple (5-block) bridge task: world, mechanics, predicates, processes, oracle plan, and the partially-observable learning story.

Why another domain?

  • Suite motivation: partial observability + slow hidden processes (boil: heat, bond: bonding, ...)
  • Bridge adds the missing archetype: a hidden latent whose consequence is kinematic, not a feature readout
  • Once a glue joint cures, the two blocks are physically welded (JOINT_FIXED constraint) into one rigid assembly
  • Picking any block of the assembly transports the whole thing - the hidden latent changes what actions do
  • No other domain in the suite has that property

The world (simple variant)

Initial staging: legs standing (periwinkle, teal), spans lying (rosewood, amber, terracotta), glue bottle (white), two slate site pads mid-table

  • 5 identical 10×5×5 cm blocks: 2 "legs" stand on end, 3 "spans" lie flat - the SAME block at different orientations
  • Glue bottle: slim box, top-graspable; its tip wets faces
  • 2 site pads: slate markers mid-table where the legs must stand
  • Fetch arm in front; staging on a jittered grid split around the bridge band (two front rows + one back row)
  • Block colors draw per task from role families - legs cool blues/teals, spans warm terracotta/ambers - without replacement, so same-role blocks stay distinct
  • One collision shape, one logical block type; FULL 6D pose = (x, y, z, roll, pitch, rot) - any physical orientation is representable, nothing is silently erased at state syncs. Reads are canonicalized from the quaternion (roll folds to 0 within ~1° of the gimbal pole) and orientation checks compare geodesically, so the standing pose (pitch = -π/2, the Euler singularity) is exact

Why two site pads?

One pad per bridge leg, 25 cm apart, centered on the table

  • Symbolic goal grounding: the goal says AtSite(leg0, site0) / AtSite(leg1, site1) - without site objects there is no way to express where the bridge must stand, and PlaceLegAtSite's sampler would have no grounded target
  • Separation encodes the bridge footprint: site_sep = 0.25 is matched to the welded 3-span row (each 10 cm span seats 2.5 cm inboard of its leg, so the row's outer ends land flush over the leg tops). The row's length is frozen by the weld before seating, so the legs must already stand at exactly that separation
  • Build-order gates: SiteFree gates PlaceLegAtSite; AtSite is a SeatSpan3 precondition - without it the planner seated the span onto legs still at their staged spots and "moved" them afterwards
  • Task diversity: the pad pair's midpoint x is jittered per task (site_x_jitter), so leg placement varies instead of being memorizable

Glue mechanics (the hidden process)

Wet faces render as yellow patches: the two row ends (span0/span1 end_b) are glued

  • 3 glue-able faces per block (local frame): top, end_a/end_b (long-axis ends); a standing leg's world-top IS its end_b face - one face mechanics covers row joints and leg tops
  • Wet: bottle tip within 2 cm of a face's dab point → glue_<face> flips
  • Cure: wet face in aligned resting contact with another block (neither held) → hidden cure_<face> counter ticks each step
  • At cure_threshold = 25 the joint irreversibly latches: glue consumed, attached_* set on both blocks, physical weld created
  • Interrupting the contact (e.g. a pick mid-cure) resets the counter; the wet glue persists
  • Welds snap relative pose (yaw-only, ideal Δz) before freezing, else the assembly creeps across the table

The simple task: 5 blocks, 2 glue joints

Goal atoms pin the geometric layout + the row welds: AtSite ×2, SeatedOn ×2, NextToEnd ×2, Attached ×2 (row only). Seat joints are deliberately NOT in the goal - glue appears only where physics demands it.

Why glue? It's structural

  • The 3-span row is the minimum that makes glue structurally necessary: a 2-span row mutually supports as a friction arch (verified up to 16 cm spans), and a lone span's ~1 cm overhang is damped by rolling friction
  • The seat joints are not structural: the welded row sits stably on the legs by gravity - so they are not in the goal and the oracle never glues leg tops. Every glue joint the task uses is a physical necessity the agent can discover, never a goal decoration (the goal_nl is outcome-only: no glue recipe)
  • So the lateral welds are load-bearing in both senses: the row cannot be seated without them, and picking either end carries the whole beam

The latent is kinematic

  • The two frames are visually identical - the cure completed between them (only the attached_* features flipped)
  • Seating exploits this: grasp either span; the far span lands over its leg purely by the rigid geometry
  • The motion planner excludes welded partners of the held block from the collision set (get_welded_partner_ids)

State & predicates

Block features: full 6D pose (x y z roll pitch rot - a block "stands" iff |pitch| > π/4; a standing leg's world-top is its local end_b face), is_held, wet flags glue_top/end_a/end_b (local frame), hidden counters cure_*, partner indices attached_* (-1 = none; FO mode only - in PO mode both cure and attached ride the privileged channel), color.

Processes (oracle world model)

Endogenous (option-backed, delay μ 2-4):

  • PickBlockFromTable (gated by EndsFree + Loose: loose blocks only), PickGlueBottle
  • PickSpanFromRow (dismantles an uncured butt joint; deletes the named NextToEnd - the Unstack analog)
  • PickRow (grasp the welded row by its MIDDLE span; requires the COMPLETE Attached chain, so a half-cured row can never be lifted - lifting would dangle the welded partner and break the pending joint's resting contact)
  • PlaceLegAtSite, PlaceSpanNextTo
  • SeatSpan3 (seat the welded row across the legs: mid block to the midpoint of the leg tops)
  • ApplyGlueEndB (backs the generic MoveTo skill; the sampler aims the bottle tip at the face)
  • PlaceBlockOnTable, PlaceBottleOnTable (staging escape hatch)
  • Wait (ConstantDelay 1)

Exogenous (hidden dwell, μ = 25):

  • CureLateralJoint: GlueEndB(left) ∧ NextToEnd(right, left) ∧ both Resting → Attached(left, right)
  • The env's physics cures ANY wet face in aligned contact (top faces included) - the abstract model only names the joint the task needs
  • Condition must hold throughout the delay: a pick mid-cure kills the process, exactly like the env's counter reset
  • SeatSpan3 preconditions force the real build order: fully Attached span chain + legs AtSite (no glue conditions - seat welds are neither structural nor in the goal)
  • CureLateralJoint adds only the goal-order Attached(left, right): both orders let the planner "satisfy" the goal with a reversed, unseatable row
  • No NSRT adds Attached - the domain is solved by oracle_process_planning, not plain sesame oracle

A typical simple-variant plan

Pick(leg0)    -> PlaceLegAtSite(leg0, site0)
Pick(bottle)  -> ApplyGlueEndB(span0), ApplyGlueEndB(span1)  # row joints
              -> PlaceBottleOnTable
Pick(span1)   -> PlaceSpanNextTo(span1, span0) # first butt joint forms
Pick(leg1)    -> PlaceLegAtSite(leg1, site1)   # while the joint cures
Wait ...                                       # CureLateralJoint #1
Pick(span2)   -> PlaceSpanNextTo(span2, span1) # second butt joint forms
Wait ...                                       # CureLateralJoint #2
PickRow(mid)  -> SeatSpan3(span0..2, leg0, leg1)  # done
  • The planner interleaves work with cure delays where it can (e.g. erecting legs or parking the bottle while a joint cures)
  • cure_threshold (25) deliberately exceeds a Place's ~14 steps, so a cure never completes during the place retreat and strands the following Wait with its target already true

The partially-observable learning story

  • With CFG.partially_observable, the block type drops cure_* AND attached_* (no perception system reports "attached to block 3") - the agent sees only wet flags, poses, and the kinematic consequences, and must postulate attachment itself as a latent relation inferred from co-motion
  • True counters and attachments live env-side and in state.privileged, so planner backtracking restores each search node's own hidden state (and goal checking stays exact)
  • GT simulator program (gt_simulator_po.py): Pattern A - recurrent hidden counter (latent["cure"]) whose latch writes latent["attached"]; the weld rule re-emits every latent pair as an Attach physics command - the latch's only observable footprint. Fit params: cure_threshold, apply_glue_radius, contact tolerances
  • The threshold is identifiable from when joints latch across trajectories; gates are hard → the recurrent fit is gradient-free

So the agent starts from: raw PO observations, the 5 skills, Holding, and a sentence describing the bridge - and must invent the predicates, ground the goal, and identify the hidden curing process.

Design decisions worth knowing

  • Tolerances are asymmetric on purpose: a lateral placement error is frozen into the weld and shifts where the far span meets its leg, so the perp tolerance (3 cm) exceeds real place accuracy - a 2 cm gate left bridges with a seat joint that could never cure
  • Weld strength 10000 N: PyBullet's default 500 sags under a cantilevered span
  • Staging is a jittered grid, not rejection sampling: the 6 staged objects + span-assembly strip + site keepouts don't pack randomly; the span row is assembled in place along the front row
  • Bottle parking is corridor-aware: cells to a lying span's right are future row-placement targets even though they look empty now
